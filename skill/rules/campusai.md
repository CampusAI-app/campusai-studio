# CampusAI: Jacob's preferences, in one place

**These are preferences, not hard rules (11/25).** Jacob: "I don't want those to be hard rules. I want those to be
preferences." Every line in this file, including the ones written as "never" or "always", is what he has liked or
disliked so far. Lean toward them. Break one when the film is better for it, and say which one and why when you show
the film. A note he gave about one film is a hint for the next, not a law for all of them.

Gathered 2026-10-22 from `motion-library.md` (items 7 to 9 and the 09-16 rules), the hook-films
brief and LOG. Newest wins where two disagree. Add every new note here, dated, the day he gives it. The two sections
right below (how things move, and the styles of the recent films) are the newest and lead.

## How things move (preferences, 11/25, from the Nov 24 films and his notes on them)
What he wants more of: "better animations, things on screen, not keeping your eyes moving."
- **Keep the eye in one place.** Decide where the viewer is looking and bring things to that spot, so they never
  hunt: the next line lands where the last one was, the product chip slides in under the hook, a card lifts toward
  the middle. When the eye has to move, lead it with one motion (a red outline drawing on, the camera pushing in),
  never two things appearing in two places at once. One thing moves at a time; nothing moves for a second reason
  while something else is moving (08/30). Words keep the same place from beat to beat (the keynote's line always in
  one band above the monitor).
- **Whole thing first, then bring the one part forward.** The real page at desktop size for context, then the camera
  pushes in until the thing being named fills the width (Feature Friday round 6), or the part lifts off the page to be
  read (the trial reels' red outline, then the cards lifting). Never leave a small thing on a big page to be found.
- **Show it working, not a still of it.** "When it shows the chat it should show it actually answering, not a
  screenshot" (Living campus). Jobs come as cards: "If doing the job question, show the cards" (Living campus), "when it
  shows the jobs it should show them in cards also" (Keynote). Degree progress shows the audit's own sections, "the
  degree audit section with the sections" (Keynote), not a bare percentage.
- **Quick, and more of it.** "It's a little slow" (Keynote round 2) became a new beat every 1.2 to 2.8 s. "More
  aggressively showing people, quickly" and "a better animation of showing the people" (Living campus) became faces
  strobing into a wall. Piles land fast (a page every 0.3 s in Feature Friday).
- **Things up front.** "I'd rather the cards show up at the beginning" (Feature Friday): the brief arrives with its
  cards already on it and each one lights as it is named, rather than building from nothing.
- **No empty space.** "A lot of empty space" (Keynote round 3 direction): the product stays at real size and big words
  fill the rest of the frame around it. Never pad with blank ground or shrink the product to fill a gap.
- **Words land as they are said.** Word by word at speaking pace. A line can sit faint and light up as it is spoken
  (trial reels), or build in at the top with one red word (Feature Friday).
- **Transitions carry you in.** The pile of pages collapses into one point that opens as the dashboard (Feature
  Friday); the camera pushes through the laptop's glass into the product (Who to talk to); a red flood carries into the
  end (Living campus); the UMD M glides from big to a small tag (trial reels). Not cuts to black or plain fades.
- **Hold the payoff.** The one line that matters holds long enough to read twice ("Meet CampusAI" held 2.4 s); the end
  card about 3 s.
- **Glide, never jolt.** No loading or skeleton frame on screen (Living campus round 6); a streaming chat glides; dissolve
  between states rather than playing a recorded scroll.
- **Sound lands on the motion.** A low hit on the payoff, a click on each press. Trial reels carry no music in the file:
  "take away the background music... I'd rather just choose songs on Instagram."
- **One palette per film.** "Random colors, not premium" (Living campus): a family of reds around the brand red, not
  extra colours for variety.

  the campusai wordmark with its spark for a second, the whole real page with red outlines on what the line names, then

- **Laptop at night** (Feature Friday; `src/trials/post-everything-laptop.tsx`). A real laptop on a warm dark desk with a

  named thing fills the width; a narrator the whole way. Good for: "everything in one place", setup to payoff, 40 s plus.

## Named styles are each their own world (11/08, newest, wins over the type and colour lines below and in SKILL.md)
Jacob, on the first 20 styles from the "30 Claude animations" reel: "These still look pretty similar and not actually
different", "The fonts and the backgrounds are also all the same", and "I'm trying to create these completely different
types of content that I want to be their own thing, so we have different types of things we could always post. I don't
really like the brand font, though, because it's super AI-generic and classic. We should be experimenting with different
fonts. The colors should be more red, but it shouldn't always just be a black background or a white background."
- **A named style owns its whole frame**: its own ground, palette, typeface, layout and ending. Never the shared title
  top left, the shared caption block, or the shrink into the shared end card. Two styles side by side must not be
  mistakable for each other.
- **Fonts: experiment.** Each style picks its own face (Google Fonts through `@remotion/google-fonts`), chosen for the
  style. Zodiak and Inter are no longer the default for named styles. Still no monospace and no all-caps
  wide-tracked labels.
- **Colour leans red, and grounds vary.** Reds, oxbloods, pinks, corals, terracottas, warm papers, wood, deep water at
  dusk. Not black or white by default. Brand red `#D9202E` stays the anchor, and a style may use a family of reds around it.
- **The brand lives in the logo.** The logo still arrives inside two seconds and every piece still ends on it, but drawn
  in the style's own language on the style's own ground (stamped, flapped, printed), not on the shared dark card.
- Product films, captures and chat recordings keep the product's own look; this note is about named styles.
- **A world style shows the product or makes a joke (11/17).** Jacob, on the first Brick World and CGI Calm cuts: "For
  these random types, where it's like the Lego ones and other things, we should try to be showing the product in it
  when we can, or have it be a little funny or satirical. Otherwise, it doesn't really make sense for those." A pretty
  world with an answer line on top is not enough. Put a real capture inside the world (a screen, a billboard, a phone,
  a page the plane unfolds into), or make it funny (a satirical voice, a gag that tops out on CampusAI). Both is best.
- **The planner shows the planning, never its disclaimer (11/17).** Jacob, on Brick World round 2: "it doesn't really
  show the actual planning of it out. Also, whenever you show the planner, like Degree Audit, you always show the alert
  of 'we're not the guy,' not the actual thing for Degree Audit things, which is really the main part of it." Any
  capture of the planner or degree audit frames the terms with their courses and the requirements being met. The "a
  guide, not your official degree audit" alert is never the shot; crop or scroll past it.
- **A joke has to follow from what the product really does (11/17).** CGI Calm round 2 ("Finals week. Total calm." over
  the calendar) failed: "That wouldn't just show up in the calendar." The line has to be something the screen on show
  plainly causes.
- **UMD in the first second (11/17).** Jacob: "They should try to have the UMD logo or something showing up at UMD at
  the beginning, because otherwise people might scroll through if it's not related to them." Every named style opens
  with `UmdOpen` (`src/styles/UmdOpen.tsx`): the UMD M pops in big at 0.1 s and glides into a small tag at 1.1 s, his
  Nov 16 move. The logo file is `public/private-umd-logo/umd-m.svg`, on Jacob's Mac only; a teammate without it leaves
  the M out and opens on a UMD landmark instead.

## Trial reels (11/09)

- **Web, never mobile.** "Dont use mobile, Use web instead." Show the WHOLE web page at frame width,
  with parts lifting off it to be read. A column crop reads as a phone; a laptop push-in reads as
  "showing it half at a time... makes the flow worse".
- **Stay 9:16.** Trial reels only run as Reels; a horizontal video plays as a small strip.
- **The bridge:** her words become a red tag that lands on the product. Not the student reacting.
- **Captions copy our best reels:** question title on top with a red underline, white phrase
  captions under the speaker.
- **"All in one place" opens on the daily brief** ("everything brought together"), shows more of
  Job Match and of why a job fits, and degree progress rather than semester credits or GPA.
  Section names land big ("bigger and more aggressive").
- **No Tailor resume reel yet** ("we dont have fully just yet").
- **Craft rules from three cold reviews:** set cuts and captions from measured loudness (word
  timestamps ran up to 0.8 s off and clipped words); never play a recorded `npm run flow` scroll
  (it plays at about 6 fps), dissolve between states; the phrase-to-tag morph hides the caption the
  same frame; no dead air under the product half (a soft Mixkit bed, never over the student).
- **What the numbers say so far:** opening on a person holds (44 to 55% skip); opening on the
  product or a feature loses most people in the first seconds (72 to 91% skip). The live version
  is the playbook, not this line.
- **Recent trial reels (11/24)** use the "Light trial reel" look in "Styles from the recent films" above. It is one

## The product on screen
- Real dark-mode web captures of campusai-demo (or dev or main), never a mockup, never mobile
  (parked). It must look like the WEB product at phone size: a capture that "looks like mobile,
  can't see anything" failed (10/21). Show the page big and in context.
- The look: dark screens on near-black `#0b0708` with the product's red glow; the red-black
  gradient runs through the whole piece; the product IS the frame, no visible seam or box (10/16).
- **Never show Sandy Spring** (Maya Chen's demo internship), in a film or on a captured page.
  Lean on the Smith Investment Fund for her experience (10/21, in caps).
- Never mention ChatGPT. No "Free for UMD students". Instagram endings may say "Link in bio."
- UMD's logo and UMD imagery (the turtle, campus, colours) CAN go in a video (11/04, correcting an older blanket ban
  that came from the 10/16 social graphics, where the backgrounds were cut because "it blocks out the left side"). It
  is just sometimes kept off the product itself, so ask before putting it on a product screen.
  11/10, Jacob: "Don't worry about UMD imagery in videos." Use it freely in films (campus photos, the M, real UMD
  senders like ELMS-Canvas and Careers4Terps); no need to ask. Wikimedia photos still carry their on-screen credit.
- **Capture fresh from the current demo for every film (11/16).** Captures already in `public/` go stale when a page is
  redesigned: the Goldman film used the Oct 23 Events page after Events was rebuilt. Before using a stored capture,
  open the live page and compare; if it changed, capture again.
- Every press shows where its result lands. Show, never label ("Missed" was cut).
- Capture from EMPTY: a picker starts empty and gets filled by typing or a tapped suggestion,
  never pre-filled then X'd out (10/21).
- Use different real directions in one run (investment banking, consulting, something else),
  not one track repeated (10/22).
- Explain a concept the first time it appears (what the North Star is) in one line over the
  thing itself (10/22).
- Teach by showing: scan the real posting and show the results; show the specific changes on
  THEIR resume, not a generic before/after (10/21, 10/22).
- Never a demo number that is not true on main. If the demo cannot do something yet (a re-rank),
  the film never claims it.

## Opening and ending
- **Every video opens student-first, at Maryland (11/16, Jacob: "it should be student-centric and Maryland-beginning.
  I think all of our videos should have that").** The first line is a UMD student in their own words: who they are and
  what they are after ("Junior at Maryland. Finance. Where do I even start?"), then the product answers. Never open on
  a company name or a feature. Product films keep the dark product ground below, never a paper or grid look.
- Logo near the start and always at the end (10/22). Earlier: "any cut gets a short logo
  intro" (10/16) and "logo at 0.5 s with the watermark held top-left" (Job Match, 09/16).
- The hook is in the first 0.5 s; the opening is never slow (10/21).
- **Never open on an empty screen (11/24).** Jacob: "I don't like when they start with nothing on the screen, because
  then people don't really know much... and they skip." Frame 0 already carries the hook: the first line set, the UMD M
  already up, the visual mid-motion. Animate the rest in from there, never everything in from blank. Frame 0 is also the
  thumbnail in a folder and the first thing a phone shows. Worked example: the hooks in `src/trials/post-howdo-remake.tsx`
  (lines start early, a spoken line sits faint on screen and lights up word by word as it is said).
- Orientation plays OVER the thing, not in front of it: the hook lines sit over the real card
  already on screen, never on black (09/16).
- Never open on the North Star picker for Job Match (rejected twice, 09/16). Skip greeting pages
  and retyping; open each chapter on the question (10/16).
- End by zooming into, or turning something into, the CampusAI logo. No closing slogan line. (Superseded for
  chat films 10/30: "at the end, it should say something like 'Ask CampusAI anything.'" The registration film
  ends on that line above the logo and campusai.app.)
- Films made on the film system (`studio/src/system/`) end on the logo plus "campusai.app"
  ("Link in bio" on Instagram cuts), held about 3 s (10/23, his yes on the What Ships finding:
  logo plus address beats the logo alone; a logo-first opening only works for famous brands).

## How a film gets made (10/23)
- Two modes, picked per film: "individual" shows him a grey draft (real camera, timing and text
  on grey pages) before anything polished renders; "bulk" skips the draft and goes straight to
  final. His words: "sometimes I want a lot of them made in bulk, but sometimes I work on ones
  individually." Never force the draft on a bulk batch.
- Funny "Ask CampusAI" films are welcome back, but not like bingo or Midterms Wrapped (10/23).
  The joke has to land on a real thing the product does.

- A chat answer recorded from the docked panel and blown up to 9x16 reads "stretched", "on a phone", "bad
  text". For a quick ask-and-answer beat, make it a motion graphic of the chat instead: the field zoomed in,
  the question typing, the loader, then the answers as options a cursor opens. Use the product's own words
  (placeholder, loader label, the real answer), never invented ones (10/23, study spot film). This is his
  call for that beat, not a retirement of real captures for whole product films.
- No headline over or above a product beat that competes with it: "the one text block is blocking it"
  (10/23).
- A chat recorded in a narrow window shows the product's MOBILE layout: "it's trying to be on mobile when it's
  getting it from the web" (10/29, registration films). For `npm run chat`, record the desktop web layout in a tall
  window (1100 x 1956, `zoom` 1.8 makes the conversation readable) and frame it with the camera. The 820 px size in
  `scripts/chat.mjs` flips to mobile. Keep the page's top logo row (the school's M beside ours) out of frame.
- **Desktop layout only, every format, until the mobile app ships (11/06, corrects 11/05).** On 11/05 he asked for "a mobile
  format like a real one" for reels; a day later, after seeing it: "I'd rather show it on a computer, not on a phone, because
  we're not on the phone yet either." So no phone screens and no "on your phone" copy. A vertical reel uses the tall desktop
  recording above (1100 x 1956, `zoom` 1.8), shown big with the product as the frame. Never crop a wide desktop recording
  into a narrow card ("trying to shove it into a real format").
  Saying the app is coming is allowed (11/24, Jacob on the team sitcom film: "mobile is allowed because we are working
  towards that"). Word it as in progress ("building the CampusAI app"), never as shipped. Same day, for the team
  sitcom: "I would rather show actual mobile screens that we're working on", so real screens of the mobile app in
  progress (captured from the simulator build, its sample data) are allowed where the piece is about the team building
  it. Product films still use the desktop web layout.
- **Real computer size, never a tall or narrow window (11/24, replaces the tall window in the two bullets above).** The
  1100 x 1956 window reflows the product: at `zoom` 1.8 its cards wrap a word per line, and under 1280 px some pages change
  layout (My Calendar's task list drops under the grid). Jacob on the keynote film's round 3 direction: "I don't think that's
  how it shows up on the web" and "you're trying to fit it on a phone... it makes it look weird." For a vertical reel,
  record at 1440 x 900 and show the whole screen (on a monitor in a room, as `post-keynote-monitor` round 3 does), and fill
  the rest of the 9:16 frame with big words, never with a reflowed or cropped product. A chat may still use `zoom` in
  chat.json: it enlarges only the thread and the box, and the page keeps its desktop layout.
- Show only what the product has. No map: CampusAI has none, so a map beat reads as a feature that does not exist (11/06).

## Camera and motion
- Smooth and focused: one subject, arrive early, hold, release when the beat completes. Nothing
  moves for a second reason while something is already moving (08/30).
- ONE camera framing through a continuous action. The camera never swings while typing (10/22).
- A streaming chat answer must glide, never jolt: the product scrolls the thread a line at a time, and a film
  that shows those jumps reads as "a little glitchy" (10/30, registration films). Make the recording itself glide:
  `capture/register-chat-record.mjs` turns the product's `scrollTop = scrollHeight` pin into a spring the page
  follows, so a close frame needs no correction in the edit. Typing is one new letter per film frame, about 30 a
  second: "smoother and quicker" than 20 a second, which gave one or two letters per frame unevenly (10/30).
- Two versions of a chat film for students (10/30): the plain one, and a separate "more edited up" one for a Gen Z
  feed, because the plain one "might be a little boring or slow". What he means by edited up is SMALL things: icons,
  soundtrack, noises, things popping up ("smaller things, like icons, soundtracks, noises"), not big set pieces.
  But small icons beside each line were rejected ("doesn't look good"): they only repeated the word next to them.
  What landed in `RegisterChatGenZ-16x9`: one big fact sticker per answer on a held beat, the answer dimmed, every
  word from the answer; the real AOSC123 block lifting once; the opening question over the app.
- Don't make the viewer wait on the chat: "a lot of it is just waiting for the chat to respond" (10/30). Thinking
  steps about half a second each (shorter and the product's fade stacks them unreadably), the answer streamed
  ~2.5x faster, and a close frame on the conversation while it streams.
- A zoom into the screenshot wall was rejected ("makes the rest not look so clean"); one screen
  popping out of its slot landed (10/16).
- **The pop-out** (a real piece of the product lifting off its page in 3D) is liked, not a rule.
  Use it when highlighting one thing; vary how it pops (spotlight lift, `element-body-moves`,
  `depth-layer-moves`, `magician-card-flourish`, `space-camera-moves`).
- Don't go too quick, and use the shotcraft shots rather than hand-built moves (10/21).
- A 3D ring is wrong when the real list does the job; the real list then the press (10/21).
- Films in one batch never share a signature move.
- He loves the motion and the sound of adiiguu's Island "for Agents" carousel (11/03, through Muse): one
  system with one signature move (rack focus), tempo that alternates beat to beat, and sound on the transitions.
  The breakdown and our matching parts are in `lanes/motion.md` under Reference.
- Red bars rising from the bottom on an end card read as AI: never (10/19).
- The drawn `Laptop3D` "looks fake": use `four/real-laptop.tsx` (10/19).
